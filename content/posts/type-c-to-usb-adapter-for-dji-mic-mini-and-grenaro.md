I tested it with both the DJI Mic Mini and the Grenaro wireless mic, and both work perfectly. Here's exactly what you need and how to set it up.**

### 🤔 The Problem

Modern wireless microphones like the DJI Mic Mini and Grenaro come with receivers that have a **USB-C connector**. That's fine for newer laptops, Android phones, and iPhones 15 and above. But millions of PCs — especially desktops and older laptops — only have the traditional **USB-A ports** (the rectangular ones).

If you've ever tried plugging a USB-C receiver into a USB-A port, you already know: it doesn't fit. You need an adapter.

### 🔌 How to Connect — Step by Step

The setup is plug-and-play. No drivers, no software, no configuration needed.

**1. Take the USB-C to USB-A adapter** from the cable kit.

**2. Plug the USB-C end** into your microphone receiver (DJI Mic Mini receiver or Grenaro receiver).

**3. Plug the USB-A end** into any standard USB port on your PC.

**4. Open your recording software** — OBS, Audacity, Windows Voice Recorder, or any DAW. Your mic should appear automatically as an input device.

**5. Select it as your microphone input** and you're good to go.

That's it. No pairing, no Bluetooth, no setup wizard. The receiver is recognised immediately as a USB audio device by Windows and macOS.

### 📋 What to Check Before You Buy

A few things worth confirming before you order any USB-C to USB-A adapter:

*   **OTG support** — make sure the adapter supports USB OTG (On-The-Go). Most modern adapters do, but cheap ones sometimes don't. OTG is what allows a USB-C device to act as a host when connected to a computer.
*   **USB 2.0 is enough** — audio data from a wireless mic receiver is very lightweight. You don't need USB 3.0 speed. Any USB-A port on your PC will work, including the older blue ones.
*   **No power delivery needed** — the mic receiver is powered by its own battery (or by the USB connection itself in some cases). You don't need a powered hub.

### 🖥️ Compatible Setups

This adapter approach works across:

*   **Windows PC** — desktop or laptop with USB-A ports
*   **Mac** — older MacBooks with USB-A, or via a USB-C hub with USB-A ports
*   **Linux** — recognised as a standard USB audio device, no drivers required
*   **Any device with USB-A** — including some smart TVs, recording interfaces, and camera monitors

### 💡 Pro Tip

If you're doing a proper recording setup and want to keep the receiver plugged in while also charging other devices, look for a USB hub with individual power switches. That way you can connect the mic receiver to one port and keep other ports free for storage, lighting controllers, or other peripherals — all from a single USB-A port on your laptop.

A simple, cheap adapter is all that stands between your wireless mic and your PC. No need to buy a new laptop or a dedicated audio interface just to use a USB-C microphone on an older machine.
